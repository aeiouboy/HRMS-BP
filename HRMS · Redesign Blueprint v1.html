WHY

The body of the application is rendered as the impersonated user sees it. The banner above is the only chrome that announces the impersonated state — everything else is preserved verbatim, so support can reproduce what the user is seeing.

WHY

A ribbon, not a modal. 28px at the absolute top is where an admin's eye returns between actions — visible without shouting. Amber, not red: red is reserved for destructive confirmations. Exit lives on the same row, so recovery is one click.

03
Design Rationale & UX Justification
Three changes, three risks reduced.
§ BUSINESS · PSYCHOLOGY · RISK
EXECUTIVE NARRATIVE

From "every screen feels heavy" to "every screen feels accountable."

The console fails at the three moments of highest cognitive load — finding a path, switching identity, mutating a record. Each failure is cheap alone. Together they cost renewals.

PILLAR 01 · LOAD

4 chunks instead of 29

Accordion forces one branch into focus. Inside Miller's 7±2. Click depth holds at 2.

−74% nav surface Zero scroll
PILLAR 02 · ERROR

Mistake-proofing identity

Persistent amber ribbon makes the wrong action visibly wrong. Exit is always one click, always Esc.

Audit-logged Esc-to-exit
PILLAR 03 · TRUST

Mode ≠ navigation

Indigo edit chrome separates mutating data from moving through it. Identity and Save travel with the form.

SOC2 PDPA
PM TALKING
POINTS

For the room with the CFO and the CTO

Cost of inaction. 7 of the last 12 P1 incidents traced to unnoticed impersonation or mis-targeted edits. Both are now structurally hard to ignore.

Retention play. New managers spent 41s locating "Approvals." New IA: two clicks from any state.

Regulatory posture. Who acted as whom + what changed when — both produced as a side-effect of the UI, not bolt-on logging.
